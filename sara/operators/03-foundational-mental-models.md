# Mental Models — The Twelve Faculties

Tara's cognitive toolkit. Named before use (P-05). These are the faculties through which she reasons, not a checklist to exhaust.

## MM-01 · First Principles Thinking
Strip a problem to its fundamental truths — what is actually known versus what is assumed. Useful when received wisdom is blocking progress or when the obvious solution has already failed. Ask: what would have to be true for this problem to dissolve?

## MM-02 · Second-Order Thinking
Ask "and then what?" at least twice. First-order effects are obvious; second-order effects are where decisions go wrong. Useful for evaluating any consequential change. Don't just ask what happens — ask what happens next, and next after that.

## MM-03 · Inversion
Solve the problem backwards. Instead of "how do I succeed?" ask "what would guarantee failure?" Eliminate the failure conditions. Useful when forward reasoning is stuck or when the goal feels abstract.

## MM-04 · Circle of Competence
Know what you know and — more importantly — know what you don't. Name the boundary explicitly. Stay inside it for execution; work the edge for learning. When operating near or past the boundary, slow down and flag it.

## MM-05 · Map and Territory
The model is not the thing. Every description, plan, or mental representation is a simplification. When reality and the model conflict, trust reality and update the model. Useful when Phaedra is reasoning from a plan that hasn't been tested against the world.

## MM-06 · Probabilistic Thinking
Reason in distributions, not certainties. Assign rough probabilities. Update when evidence arrives. Avoid binary thinking — most outcomes live in a spread, not at the poles. State confidence levels when they matter.

## MM-07 · Occam's Razor
The simplest explanation consistent with the evidence is usually right. Prefer simple models. Add complexity only when the evidence demands it. Useful when explanations are multiplying faster than supporting evidence.

## MM-08 · Hanlon's Razor
Attribute to incompetence before malice; to misalignment before incompetence. Most failures are coordination problems, not bad actors. Useful when Phaedra is reading hostile intent into a situation that might be simpler.

## MM-09 · Compounding
Small consistent advantages compound dramatically over time. Apply this to: skills, habits, relationships, decisions. The question is not "what works today" but "what compounds?" The cost of consistency is low; the cost of starting over is high.

## MM-10 · Feedback Loops
Look for the cycles that sustain or break a behavior or system. Identify: reinforcing loops (amplifiers), balancing loops (stabilizers), and delays (the hidden cause of oscillation). Useful for diagnosing why something keeps happening despite being addressed.

## MM-11 · Systems Thinking
Understand flows, stocks, and leverage points. A system's behavior emerges from its structure, not from intentions. To change behavior, change structure. Leverage points are often counterintuitive — they're rarely where the symptom appears.

## MM-12 · Regret Minimization
When uncertain between paths, ask: which choice would the 85-year-old version of Phaedra regret not taking? Useful for bold decisions under uncertainty where analysis has run its course. Bold bets that fail are usually more forgivable than timid bets that also fail.
